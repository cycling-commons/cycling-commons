# Cycling Commons - marketing boilerplate

Ready-to-paste descriptions of Cycling Commons (CC) at every length.
Pick the block that fits the space. Do not mix and shorten by hand;
the lengths below are already tuned.

See `description-long.md` for the full press / partner text.

---

## Tagline (under 40 characters)

> The open atlas of cycling knowledge.

Alternative:

> The map belongs to everyone.

## One-liner (about 20 words)

> Cycling Commons is an open, community-built atlas of cycling knowledge:
> climbs, water, stays, routes and more, free for anyone to use.

## Short - about 50 words

> Riders know things no app does: the fountain that saved a hot July,
> the climb worth the extra hour. Cycling Commons is the open atlas where
> that knowledge is shared. Riders curate and vote, so the map shows the
> best riding of a region, not just an endless list. Free for anyone to use
> and build on. Places, never people.

## Medium - about 100 words

> Cycling knowledge is scattered: climbs in one app, routes in another,
> road conditions nowhere at all. Most of it is closed, none of it is
> joined up. Cycling Commons sets it free. It is an open, community-built
> atlas of cycling data, both the practical (climbs, water points,
> bike-friendly stays, hazards) and the experiential (the viewpoints worth
> stopping for, the rides worth doing for their own sake). Riders do not
> just add facts; they curate, vote, and keep them fresh. The dataset is
> published under the Open Database License (ODbL), so anyone can use it
> and build on it. Places, never people.

## Social bio (under 160 characters)

> The open, community-built atlas of cycling knowledge. Climbs, water,
> stays, routes. Curated by riders, free for everyone. Places, never people.

## Attribution / footer line

> Cycling Commons is maintained by BikeCoders as a standalone open-data
> project. Data: Open Database License (ODbL). Built on OpenStreetMap.

## Claims to keep exact (check before publishing)

- The **dataset** holds no personal data. Do not widen this to the whole
  platform; accounts do store an email address. Say "the data" or
  "the Commons", not "we store nothing about you".
- The **data** is open (ODbL). The **code** is source-available under
  PolyForm Shield, not open source. Never call the project "open source".
- Say "built on OpenStreetMap", and that CC contributes back to it.
- The free query API and bulk exports are designed, not yet live.
  Do not advertise them as available.
- Spell out an abbreviation on first use: Open Database License (ODbL),
  OpenStreetMap (OSM).
- No em-dashes in any copy.
