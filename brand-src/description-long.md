# Cycling Commons - long description

Full-length text for press pages, partner intros, directory listings,
and anywhere a real story fits. About 350 words. For shorter blocks,
use `boilerplate.md`.

---

Every rider carries a private map: the fountain that saved a hot July,
the climb worth the extra hour, the back road that is somehow always
empty. Today that knowledge lives in one head and dies there. Cycling
Commons is where it lives instead.

Cycling Commons is an open, community-built atlas of cycling knowledge.
It gathers both the practical and the experiential: climbs, water
points, bike-friendly stays, hazards and road conditions on one side;
the viewpoints worth stopping for, the heritage worth a detour, and the
rides worth doing for their own sake on the other. The data is
published under the Open Database License (ODbL), free for anyone to
use, build on, and improve. Even commercial apps can build on the
Commons and contribute back, but the Commons is independent and built
to outlive any single app.

The problem it answers is simple. Cycling knowledge is scattered:
climbs in one app, routes in another, places to sleep in a third, road
conditions nowhere at all. Most of it is closed, and none of it is
joined up. OpenStreetMap (OSM) is open, but raw and thin on the
cycling-specific layers that matter. The real value of the Commons is
the curation layer on top. Riders do not just contribute facts; they
curate, vote, and keep them fresh, adding the cycling judgment that raw
data lacks and closed apps lock away. So the Commons is not just an
endless list; it answers the question a rider actually asks: what is
the best riding here?

One principle is not negotiable: the Commons is the map, not the rider.
Everything in the dataset is about the world. A person's identity,
history, fitness metrics, and movements never enter it. The map is
public; the person is not.

Cycling Commons builds on OpenStreetMap and contributes durable facts
back to it. It is not a company's database with an open license bolted
on; it is a governed commons, designed so it cannot be enclosed later,
even by the people who started it.

Cycling Commons is maintained by BikeCoders as a standalone open-data
project.
