# Moderator rulebook

The short, always-current rules for everyone holding a curator or moderator
role. If a situation isn't covered here, ask in the curator channel before
acting — this page is the do's and don'ts, not a replacement for judgement.

## The three verbs — and what each one keeps

| Verb | When | What happens | What is kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Approve** | The contribution is plausible and useful | It goes live; changes apply to the item | Everything, plus your decision in the item's visible history |
| **Reject** | Honest contribution, but wrong / not verifiable / duplicate | Nothing goes live; the rider gets your note | The submission and any photos, for the standard retention window (currently 3 months), then collected |
| **Trash** | Spam, abuse, or **illegal content** | Immediate permanent deletion — content, photos, and their history are gone at once, no retention window, and **no message is sent** to the submitter | Only a **content-free record of the action**: that *you* trashed submission N of type X at time T. The content itself is unrecoverable — deliberately |

**Keep the record, never the content** is the Trash principle: the Commons
can always prove the moderation action happened, and can never reproduce
the material — which is the point when the material is spam or illegal.

Because Trash is irreversible, the desk asks you to **type `DELETE`** to
confirm. Cancelling leaves no trace at all — no record is written for a
Trash you didn't complete.

## Do

- **Do use Reject for disagreement, Trash only for bad faith.** A wrong
  location or an unverifiable claim is a rejection with a friendly note —
  never a trash.
- **Do ask before deciding** when a submission is unclear: send the rider a
  message (the needs-info flow). Most "wrong" submissions are just missing
  context.
- **Do write a decision note** on rejections — the rider reads it, and it's
  the difference between a contributor who improves and one who leaves.
- **Do check a photo's context line** (capture date, distance from the pin)
  before judging it — those facts are extracted for exactly this.
- **Do decide photos individually** when a submission's facts are right but
  one photo isn't — approving the submission doesn't force approving every
  photo.
- **Do report illegal content patterns** to the site owner after trashing —
  Trash removes the content; repeated sources may need an account-level
  response, which is the admin desk's job, not yours.

## Don't

- **Don't share, download, or link pending content** anywhere outside the
  moderation desk. Unapproved material is unpublished by design; its URLs
  exist only in your queue.
- **Don't trash what you merely disagree with.** Trash destroys the
  evidence of its own correctness — use it only where keeping the content
  would itself be the harm (spam, abuse, illegal material).
- **Don't decide your own submissions** or those of people close to you —
  hand them to another curator.
- **Don't promise outcomes** in rider messages ("this will be approved once
  you…") — you're describing the process, not pre-deciding it.
- **Don't work around the tools** (no manual database edits, no editing
  content on a rider's behalf beyond what the desk offers). If the tool
  can't express the right decision, that's a bug to report, not a rule to
  bend.

## Privacy specifics

- A photo's **GPS coordinates are never stored** — you see only the
  computed distance to the pin, and only until you decide.
- Rider identity stays out of public surfaces unless the rider opted into a
  public profile; the desk may show you more than the public ever sees —
  treat everything on the desk as confidential.
- Consent records (the CC BY-SA donation a rider granted) are permanent and
  none of your concern to manage — the system enforces them.

## Where the deep rules live

This page is the operating summary. The binding contracts are the
moderation and contribution specification (retention, Trash, messages,
scoping) and the photo-uploads specification (media lifecycle) in the
project repository — when this page and a spec disagree, the spec wins and
this page has a bug.
