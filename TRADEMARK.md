# Cycling Commons Trademark Policy

The Cycling Commons software is free software under the
[GNU AGPL v3](LICENSE). The data is open under the ODbL. The wiki is open under
CC BY-SA. **The name, the logo and the wordmark are not.** This page says what
you may do with them.

An open licence on the code is not a licence to the marks. AGPL v3 section 7(e)
expressly allows a project to keep its trademark rights while giving away the
software, and that is what this policy does. You have every freedom the AGPL
grants you over the code. The name stays with the project.

Why bother: the marks are how a rider knows a map is *this* Commons, with this
data, this curation and this privacy promise. If anyone could ship anything
under the name, the name would stop meaning anything, and the rider would be the
one misled.

## 1. What this policy covers

Together, "the Marks":

- The name **Cycling Commons**.
- The **logo** (the mark itself) and the **wordmark**.
- Their source artwork and derived share images, held in `brand-src/`,
  `web/assets/brand/`, `web/public/brand/` and `atlas/demo/brand/`.
- The domain **cyclingcommons.org** and its subdomains.

Those files carry the SPDX identifier `LicenseRef-CyclingCommons-Brand` and are
listed in [`REUSE.toml`](REUSE.toml). Their text is in
[`LICENSES/LicenseRef-CyclingCommons-Brand.txt`](LICENSES/LicenseRef-CyclingCommons-Brand.txt).

**Not covered, and free for you to use:** the application icons, favicon and web
manifest. They are interface furniture, not the mark, and a fork needs them to
run. They are AGPL like the rest of the code.

## 2. What you may do without asking

This is **nominative use**: using our name to talk truthfully about us. No
permission needed.

- Say that your product works with, reads from, imports, exports to, or is built
  on the Cycling Commons.
- Say truthfully that your fork or your service is **derived from** the Cycling
  Commons source code. For example: *"based on the Cycling Commons platform"*.
- Link to us, write about us, teach with us, review us, criticise us.
- Name us in articles, talks, papers, job adverts and CVs.
- Reproduce the logo unaltered when illustrating an article or a talk about the
  project.

Three conditions apply to all of the above:

1. **Use no more of the Marks than you need.** Our name in a sentence, not our
   logo on your masthead.
2. **Do not alter the logo or wordmark.** No recolouring, no stretching, no
   cropping, no combining it with your own mark.
3. **Do not imply endorsement, affiliation, partnership or sponsorship.** If a
   reasonable reader could think we vetted, backed or run your thing, the use is
   not nominative.

## 3. What needs written permission

- Naming a **fork, modified version, distribution or hosted service** using the
  Marks, in whole or in part.
- Using the Marks in the name of any **product, service, company, application,
  domain name, social account or user group**.
- Using the logo or wordmark as your own **icon, favicon, app icon or avatar**.
- **Merchandise** of any kind.
- **Altering** the logo or wordmark, or creating a derivative mark.
- Registering the Marks, or anything confusingly similar, as a trademark or a
  domain, anywhere.

Ask first: **info@cyclingcommons.org**. Say what you want to do, where it will
appear, and for how long. We answer.

## 4. If you fork

The AGPL gives you the right to fork the code, change it, run it and pass it on.
It gives you no right to the name. So when you distribute a modified version:

1. **Give it your own name.** Not "Cycling Commons", and not a name a rider
   could confuse with it.
2. **Remove or replace the brand files.** Everything carrying
   `LicenseRef-CyclingCommons-Brand`. Grep for it, or read the block in
   `REUSE.toml`.
3. **Keep the attribution honest.** You may and should say what your work is
   derived from. *"Foo Atlas, based on the Cycling Commons platform"* is
   accurate, welcome, and needs no permission. *"Cycling Commons Foo Edition"*
   is not.
4. **Keep the AGPL obligations.** Including section 13: if you run it as a
   network service, your users must be offered your Corresponding Source.

This is the same bargain the Linux kernel, Firefox and Nextcloud strike. Free
code, reserved name.

## 5. Community and non-commercial use

Cycling clubs, local groups, mapping parties and meetups may use the name and
the unaltered logo to run and publicise **an event or a group about the Cycling
Commons**, with no fee and no paperwork, provided section 2's three conditions
hold and the event is not a paid product. Tell us at info@cyclingcommons.org so
we can help rather than because we require it.

## 6. Data, media and the wiki are a different question

This policy is about the **Marks** only.

- **Data** is ODbL. Use it commercially, attribute it, share alike the database.
  See [`licenses/COMMONS-DATA-LICENSE.md`](licenses/COMMONS-DATA-LICENSE.md).
- **Media** is CC BY-SA 4.0. See
  [`licenses/COMMONS-MEDIA-LICENSE.md`](licenses/COMMONS-MEDIA-LICENSE.md).
- **Wiki prose** is CC BY-SA 4.0.
- **Code** is AGPL-3.0-only. See [`LICENSE`](LICENSE).

Using the data does not require our permission and never will. Using the name
does.

## 7. Changes and enforcement

This policy may change. The version in the repository at the time you act is the
one that applies to you. Changes are not retroactive.

We would always rather send an email than a letter. If you think you may be over
a line, write to **info@cyclingcommons.org** and ask. Almost every case is
solved by a rename or a sentence of clarification.
